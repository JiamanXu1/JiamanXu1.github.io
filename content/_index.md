          detail_title: >-
            From Dislocation to Creativity: Reflections on Developing a Twine Game Based on Tan’s *The Strangers*
          summary: >-
            I reflect on the process of deconstructing Shaun Tan’s The Arrival (2006) through game development, the choice of Twine as the medium for creative game-making, and subsequent reflections on how the creation of these materials is deliberately structured for players to develop their game literacy. When designing the game, my aim was to express the theme of dislocation and realise it through integrating game mechanics and achieving recreation in the player’s experience. Drawing inspiration from Tan’s The Arrival, I realised that the concept of dislocation is not merely conveyed through textual narration; rather, it is embodied in abstract visual imagery, dramatic scripting and dynamic scene transitions. Consequently, I structured the game’s deeper significance around elements of partial physical isolation, cultural estrangement and symbolic interpretation. Similar to the protagonist in Tan’s work, players enter an unfamiliar environment where the language, grammar and characters are all foreign to them (Tan, 2006). Their sole means of progression is through repeated encounters and efficient reasoning of fragmented texts, ultimately decoding the true narrative hidden behind various exploratory scenes. In doing so, players engage in affective labour resulting in a profound sense of cultural immersion (Tan, 2006).
          detail: |-
            Creativity is a multifaceted concept defined as the interaction between aptitude, process, and environment through which individuals or groups produce a novel and useful product within a social context (Plucker et al., 2004, p. 90). This definition encompasses four key elements of creativity: 'person', 'process', 'product' and 'press' (environment). I use Rhodes's Four P's model of creativity (Rhodes, 1961) to explain my recreation of the game, albeit with different meanings assigned to each aspect.

            The 'person' aspect focuses on the aptitudes associated with creativity, such as enhancing players' problem-solving and reasoning abilities through gameplay (Runco & Chand, 1995). The 'process' aspect concerns the cognitive and behavioural steps involved in creative thinking and action (Wallach & Kogan, 1965). This is evident in the initial design phase, where I determine the game's narrative flow, conditional 'if' structures and potential outcome pathways.

            The 'product' aspect concerns the usability of the final artefact. In the context of a game, this is achieved through simplified UI design, clearer branching options and richer multimodal representations. Finally, the 'press' aspect considers the influence of society and culture on creativity (Glăveanu, 2015). During production, I incorporate personal experience and cultural background into the creative process (McCallum, 2012).

            Reflecting on my workflow and design decisions, I have organised the Four P's into two categories: establishing game grammar and discourse with a focus on person and press. The second part focuses on process and product, and involves the selection of development tools and the design of game semiosis and ludosis.

          title: >-
            Informal learning in self-theory through Romantic Video games: How Game Engagement Predicts Self-Esteem via Avatar Identification
          detail_title: 从角色认同到平台化亲密：乙女游戏研究的理论深化与未来路径
          summary: >-
            With the advancement of internet technology and the popularity of mobile online games, an increasing number of female users have begun to engage with video games as players. The evolution of online games has enhanced features of immersive communication, allowing players to become more deeply absorbed in the information content constructed within the virtual environment. This strengthens players’ sense of immersion and realism, enabling them to more fully adopt the perspective of the game’s protagonist. The emergence and popularity of romantic video games (RVGs) stem from female players’ expectations regarding intimate relationships and personal growth, reflecting the exercise of player agency driven by personal interest. Guided by social constructivist discourse, this study explores how players utilize romantic video games as informal learning environments. Through immersion in the game's narrative (game engagement), acceptance of perspective framing (avatar identification), and interaction with other elements within this space, players gradually come to recognize and develop their identity using templates provided by RVGs. Unlike the prevailing focus in current otome game research on identity development through interaction with others, this paper argues that while external feedback—such as social tools, methods, and pathways—can facilitate cognitive development, it is not the primary factor. Only through the subjective agency of ‘being’, as highlighted in Hall’s (1996) theory, can individuals achieve ‘becoming’, thereby extending cognition from ‘avatars’ to the ‘self’. This study establishes a mediating model linking ‘RVG engagement’ to ‘avatar identification’, and further to the ‘development of self-theory and self-esteem’ as a framework for investigation. The research was structured around three questions examining the feasibility of this model, employing thematic analysis for evaluation. Semi-structured interviews were conducted with five randomly selected highly engaged RVG players, and the responses were systematically organized and analyzed. The results indicate that the proposed mediating model is partially valid. Although players generally recognized the potential educational value of RVGs for personality development, their perceptions of the role of avatars varied significantly. While all participants reported that avatars enhanced their sense of involvement and served as motivation for engaging in parasocial relationships within the game, the degree of avatar identification differed widely among individuals due to factors such as stage of game participation, design of the avatars, and personal attitudes toward the protagonist. Additionally, from a social constructivist perspective, this study identifies a lack of clear and comprehensive definitions for key concepts such as ‘game engagement’ and ‘avatar identification’, highlighting a need for further conceptual refinement in future research.
          detail: |-
            本研究以“游戏投入—角色认同—自我理论与自尊发展”为基本分析路径，尝试说明乙女游戏如何成为女性玩家探索自我、练习亲密关系并获得情感支持的非正式学习空间。然而，访谈结果并未呈现一条稳定、单向的心理作用链条：玩家并不总是通过高度认同女主角而获得自我成长，她们也可能与角色保持距离、拒绝预设行为，或在不同情节和角色路线之间形成流动、碎片化乃至相互冲突的身份经验。这一发现提示我们，角色不应仅被理解为连接游戏投入与现实自我的透明中介，而应被视为玩家持续协商自我边界的关系性界面。借用Hall关于身份是不断“生成”而非固定“存在”的观点，以及Ihde人—技术关系理论中的具身关系、诠释关系与他异关系，玩家与角色之间可以被理解为一种动态切换的关系：角色有时是身体和情感的延伸，有时是被观察、评价和抵抗的“他者”，有时又成为玩家重新解释现实自我与理想自我差距的镜面。因此，乙女游戏的意义并不在于它是否简单提高了玩家的自尊，而在于它提供了怎样的符号、规则和关系情境，使玩家能够试演“我是谁”“我希望如何被对待”以及“我愿意成为怎样的人”。

            在此基础上，未来研究应从个体心理效果进一步转向“平台化亲密”的整体结构。近年来的游戏研究已开始使用数字亲密素养、情感基础设施和后数字亲密等概念，考察亲密关系如何同时由文本、角色、界面、算法、社群实践与物质消费共同构成。乙女游戏中的每日签到、好感度系统、抽卡和限时活动、语音来电、拟实时陪伴、社交媒体讨论、同人创作以及周边消费，并非彼此分离的附加内容，而是共同组织玩家情感节奏的基础设施。按照Nieborg和Poell的文化生产平台化理论，游戏公司不仅生产故事，也借助持续更新、数据反馈与商业机制管理玩家的注意力和情感投入。Illouz的“情感资本主义”视角看，“被理解”“被陪伴”和“值得被爱”等体验既可能成为女性确认主体价值的资源，也可能被转换为留存、付费和数据价值。Gill的后女性主义理论则进一步揭示了其中的矛盾：游戏赋予玩家选择男性角色、支配叙事进程和表达女性欲望的表面主动权，但这种主动权仍可能被预设剧情、理想化男性凝视和消费逻辑所限定。由此，乙女游戏既不是天然赋权女性的解放空间，也不能被简单归结为制造情感幻觉的商业工具。它更像是一个赋权、规训、愉悦与商品化同时发生的矛盾场域。

            未来的乙女游戏受众研究因而需要把分析单位由单一的“玩家—角色”关系扩展为“玩家—女主角—可攻略角色—平台—社群—商业机制”组成的关系网络，并通过纵向访谈、多点数字民族志、游戏内行为记录和退游者研究，追踪游戏经验如何进入玩家的日常生活。尤其值得关注的是：不同人生阶段、亲密关系经历和性别认同的玩家，是否会形成不同的角色认同路径。社群支持何时能够将游戏中的价值确认转化为现实中的表达能力和边界意识，何时又会演变为圈层压力与情感劳动。生成式对话和AI陪伴逐渐进入恋爱游戏后，虚拟角色将从预先编写的拟社会对象转变为能够持续回应和适应玩家的关系行动者，这又将如何重构亲密关系的互惠性、真实性与伦理边界。相较于继续追问“乙女游戏是否提升女性自尊”，更具有解释力的问题应是：在什么设计、社群和社会条件下，游戏中的“我值得被爱”能够沉淀为持久的自我效能与关系判断能力。又在什么条件下，它会转化为情感依赖、现实落差和持续消费。由此，乙女游戏研究才能从对玩家沉浸和情感满足的描述，推进到对数字时代女性主体性、亲密关系和平台权力之间复杂关系的解释。
